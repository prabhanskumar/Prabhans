Sound Effects (SFX) by Scene/Transition We specify per transition or scene: - **Scene transitions:** Typically use quick “whoosh” or “swoosh” effects for cuts. For example, a short *“whoosh”* at 0:15 (titles→montage), 1:15 (montage→interview), 2:45 (interview→action), and 4:15 (action→credits). Use **Mixkit’s free whoosh SFX** (all free under the Mixkit license). For instance, *“Arrow Whoosh”* or *“Cinematic Whoosh Transition”* from Mixkit, ~1–3 s long. Layer at about –6 dB (peak ~–3 dB) under the cut. - **Scene 2 (Montage) ambience:** Layer subtle ambient nature sounds (birds chirping, wind) throughout. E.g. *“Nature Ambience”* (Pixabay SFX, CC0). Play at low level (–20 to –18 dB) as background under music. - **Scene 3 (Interview background):** Add room tone or soft “office hum.” (e.g. low-volume *“indoor murmur”* from Pixabay) to fill silence. Keep very quiet so dialogue stands out. - **Scene 4 (Action):** Use specific impacts. For a fight punch at 3:00, layer a *“Punch Impact”* SFX (Pixabay “Hard Punch SFX”) at cut (–10 dB). For footsteps or mechanical sounds, use corresponding SFX (Pixabay has free categories). Level SFX higher than music (–10 to –8 dB peaks) to emphasize action. - **Scene 5 (Credits):** Possibly no SFX, or a very subtle ambient bed (e.g. soft wind or distant thunder at –25 dB). *Layering:* Follow industry practice: dialogue ~–12 dB, music ~–18 dB, SFX ~–10 dB peaks. Duck or sidechain music under dialogue when needed. All chosen SFX come from royalty-free libraries. Mixkit’s SFX are free under their license. Pixabay SFX (nature, crowd, punch, etc.) are free to use without attribution. ## 4. These free image sites have millions of CC0 images. - **Mockup Descriptions:** Suppose the video’s Scene 2 has a shot of trees: we might replace it with **before/after** “green forest at dawn” (Unsplash) to better convey morning freshness matching the upbeat music. Effort, Time, and Tools - **Effort Estimate:** 10–20 hours of work. This includes ~1–2 hours scene/audio analysis, 3–5 hours music/SFX selection and editing per scene, and ~2–4 hours of audio mixing and refining. - **Recommended Tools:** - Video Editor: Adobe Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve (for timeline editing, crossfades, color grading). - DAW/Audio Editor: Ableton Live, Logic, or Adobe Audition for precise audio editing, tempo/key adjustments, and mixing levels. - Libraries: Built-in stock libraries (Premiere’s Essential Sound panel), plus **Mixkit**, **Pixabay**, **Freesound.org** for SFX, and **Bensound/Pixabay** for music tracks. (Freesound is a CC-licensed SFX community.) - Plugins: iZotope RX for noise reduction (if needed), and VST tuners/tempo analyzers (e.g. Mixed In Key) for matching BPM. - **PySceneDetect** (Python) or FFmpeg for batch scene detection. Adjustments like tempo matching (time-stretch) and key shifting can be done via the DAW (e.g. Ableton’s Warp, Audition’s Stretch). For quick SFX mixing, consider an audio middleware or bus routing to set consistent levels.
